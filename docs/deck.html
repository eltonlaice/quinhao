The share that is owed to someone by right.

Mozambican law sends 2.75% of the mining and petroleum production tax to the communities that host the extraction. This shows where it went — and what nobody can explain.

1

The problem

The money arrives. The explanation does not.

“the budget execution report does not allow us to determine what criterion was used to split the amount allocated to each community, nor what activities the funds went to”

EITI Mozambique — the official transparency body — in its 2020 and 2021 reports

This is not an accusation from outside. It is the state's own transparency mechanism reporting that it cannot answer the question.

4

Who this is for

56 localities. The people who live in them.

Residents

Nyamanhumbir, Benga, Topuito, Pande and 52 more. They can see what was booked in their name, and who is answerable for it.

Local councils

Locality Consultative Councils approve the projects. They rarely have the allocation figures in front of them.

Journalists & civil society

CIP and others analyse the 2.75% today by reading PDFs. This makes six years queryable.

The design assumes a basic phone on a weak connection, varied literacy, and Portuguese — not a laptop in Maputo.

5

The solution

One page. Three questions answered.

  • How much came to my locality? Six years, by year, with the commodity named.
  • How does that compare? Province and district maps, filterable by year — 86.7 of Tete's 98.0 million lands in one district, Moatize.
  • What do I do now? The District Secretariat and the Consultative Council are answerable; the law limits spending to education, health, agriculture and forestry; and three specific questions a resident has the right to ask.

English, Portuguese and French. Meticais or dollars. 80 KB, no external requests, works offline once opened.

6

Why the numbers can be trusted

The build fails rather than publish a wrong figure.

  • Nothing typed by hand — five source PDFs parsed by script
  • Every year's total asserted against the published total
  • An unmatched district name fails the build instead of vanishing from the map
  • Manual corrections listed explicitly, with reasons
  • Currency converted at each year's own official rate

What the data cannot show

Execution is reported at ~100%. That means the money left the treasury — not that anything was built. The page says so, in the same type size as the good news.

Sources: EITI Mozambique · geoBoundaries · World Bank PA.NUS.FCRF

7

How AI coding tools were used

The most useful thing it did was kill the first idea.

  • Tested the premise. The original concept was "allocated vs. actually transferred". The annexes show 100% execution everywhere — that story did not exist.
  • PDF archaeology. Found the transfer tables across five reports in three different layouts, including project-level spending nobody had extracted.
  • Parsers written against a hard check, corrected until all five totals matched.
  • Verified rather than assumed. Checked data availability before committing — the national procurement portal was returning a database error, which is why this uses EITI data. Rejected an offered photograph showing a rig type Mozambique does not have.
8

Scale

The format is the same in 50+ countries.

EITI reporting is standardised. The parsing approach, the verification gate and the page all port to any implementing country that publishes subnational transfers — Ghana, Nigeria, Tanzania, the DRC.

Inside Mozambique the next layer is field verification: the page already ends by telling a resident who is answerable and what to ask. Collecting their answers turns 56 localities into a monitoring network.

Cost to run: zero. A static page and a script that re-runs when the next EITI report is published.
