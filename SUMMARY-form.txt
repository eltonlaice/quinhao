TRACK: Transparency & Accountability

Live page: https://eltonlaice.github.io/quinhao/
Repository: https://github.com/eltonlaice/quinhao

WHAT IT DOES

Mozambican law directs 2.75% of the mining and petroleum production tax to the communities
that host the extraction. The money is transferred and reported as fully executed. What is
missing is anything a resident can act on: why their locality received that particular
amount, and whether anything was built with it.

Quinhao turns six years of those transfers into a page a person in Benga or Nyamanhumbir can
read and question. It covers 56 localities across all 10 provinces, in English, Portuguese
and French, with a metical/dollar toggle. The whole page is one 80 KB file with no external
requests and no frameworks, so it loads on a weak connection and works offline once opened.

INFORMATION SOURCES

Everything comes from EITI Mozambique reports published at eiti.org - five PDFs covering six
fiscal years: the 2020 report (Table 42), the 2021 report (Table 46), the 2022 report
(Table 45, project level), and the 2023-2024 report (Annexes 6.1 and 6.2). Supporting data:
administrative boundaries from geoBoundaries, and official annual average exchange rates from
the World Bank (indicator PA.NUS.FCRF).

APPROACH TO TRUST AND ACCURACY

The subject is the credibility of public numbers, so the tool has to be checkable rather than
trusted.

Nothing is typed by hand - a script parses each source PDF, and the CSVs in the repository
are build outputs. The build fails rather than publishes a wrong number: each year's computed
total is asserted against the total printed in the source (88.0, 73.4, 44,608,666.89, 77.1
and 318.7 million meticais, plus 840.30 for the provincial annex). The same gate applies to
the map - an unmatched district name fails the build instead of silently disappearing.

Where the source PDFs merge cells and column position is ambiguous, the affected rows are
listed in a CORRECTIONS table in the code with the reason stated, rather than hidden in a
heuristic. Currency is converted at each year's own official rate, never at today's.

What the data cannot show is stated on the page, not buried: execution is reported at around
100%, which means the money left the treasury, not that anything was built. The strongest
finding is EITI's own sentence, quoted directly: the budget execution report does not allow
anyone to determine what criterion split the money between communities.

WHAT IT SHOWS

In 2024 the affected communities received 318.7 million meticais while provincial directorates
received 840.3 million. The 2020 report shows 2.75% of the 2018 production tax should have
been 100.59 million while 87.8 million was allocated - a 12.79 million gap. The money is
concentrated: 86.7 of Tete's 98.0 million lands in one district, Moatize.

USE OF AI TOOLS

The project was built with Claude Code. The page itself runs no model - the AI work is in the
build.

The most useful thing it did was kill my original idea. I set out to show "allocated versus
actually transferred"; reading the annexes showed execution reported at 100% everywhere, so
that story did not exist. The finding that replaced it came out of the documents, not a prompt.

It then located the transfer tables across five reports in three different layouts, including
project-level spending in the 2022 report; wrote one parser per year and corrected them until
all five published totals matched; and caught real errors that only surfaced because an
assertion failed, such as "Alto Moloucue" against "Alto Molocue". Data availability was
verified before committing to the idea - the national procurement portal was returning a
database error, which is why this project uses EITI data.

LIMITS

The 2022 project-level file needs a manual pass over its 41 rows and is marked as a draft.
There is no field-verification layer yet - which is why the page ends by telling a resident
who is answerable, what the law permits the money to be spent on, and three questions they
have the right to ask.
